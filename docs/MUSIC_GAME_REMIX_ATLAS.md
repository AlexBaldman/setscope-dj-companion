# Music Game Remix Atlas

SetScope should borrow proven interaction grammars from music games, arcades,
puzzle games, toys, instruments, and science exhibits, then transform them into
original musical experiences. The standard is not novelty alone: every mode
should make a musical relationship easier to hear, see, feel, perform, or create.

## The Remix Rule

Borrow the grammar, invent the sentence:

- Keep familiar verbs such as follow, dodge, match, rotate, stack, conduct,
  scratch, sequence, and improvise.
- Replace copyrighted songs, characters, layouts, art, names, and signature
  presentation with original material and SetScope's own visual language.
- Let assistance reveal the musical idea, then gradually remove it.
- Turn every successful lesson into something the player can remix or save.
- Feed performance evidence into the Skill Constellation instead of isolating
  progress inside separate minigames.

## Flagship Mode Families

### 1. Pocket Highway

A lane-based rhythm game where the target is expressive pocket, not merely
hitting a marker. Notes have signed early/late zones, velocity silhouettes, swing
curves, and ghost-note opacity. The highway bends toward the drummer's measured
latency and narrows only as timing stabilizes.

Remixes:

- separate kick, snare, hat, and sample lanes
- bass-and-drum interlock where two lanes magnetize when they lock
- DJ crossfader lane layered over pad performance
- “behind, centered, ahead” pocket studies

### 2. Beat Rescue

Fast cinematic musical situations use short controller patterns as decisions.
Instead of arbitrary button prompts, every sequence is a real rhythm, fill,
chord, or scratch phrase. Missing a prompt changes the arrangement rather than
simply ending the scene.

Remixes:

- save a collapsing cypher by trading two-bar phrases
- rebuild a dropped beat by restoring kick, snare, bass, then sample
- escape a rhythmic maze by recognizing subdivisions
- cooperative roles where players hold pulse, harmony, and melody

### 3. Sample Alchemy

Players receive an unfamiliar original crate, discover its usable fragments, and
build a beat under constraints. Rhythm Roulette supplies the discovery ritual;
the Loop Studio supplies chopping, pitching, reversing, filtering, and arranging.

Remixes:

- blind dig with one reroll
- “three sounds only” economy challenge
- transform one source into drums, bass, and texture
- call-and-response chop battles
- historical production lenses explained through techniques rather than copied
  recordings

### 4. Harmony Blocks

Falling and rotating shapes represent notes, intervals, and chord tones. Good
voice leading makes structures settle with less movement; unstable tones create
tension that can be resolved in multiple musical ways.

Remixes:

- rotate chord inversions to fit a register
- clear rows by resolving tendency tones
- reharmonize a melody without colliding with its essential notes
- build polychords by combining compatible shapes
- hear every move before confirming it

### 5. Groove Garden

Patterns grow as the player maintains pulse and variation. Repetition creates
structure; tasteful mutations create new branches; overcrowding reduces clarity.
The garden is a system visualization, not a claim that plants literally encode
music.

Remixes:

- roots are kick and bass
- stems are pulse and meter
- leaves are hats and percussion
- flowers are fills and melodic hooks
- pruning becomes arrangement and mute practice

### 6. Orbit Choir

Notes and rhythmic cycles orbit a shared center. Ratios such as 2:3, 3:4, and 4:5
become visible phase relationships. Players tap, sing, or play when bodies align,
then alter one orbit to hear the resulting polyrhythm or harmony.

Remixes:

- polyrhythm alignment challenges
- overtone-series constellations
- phase-shifting loops
- Euclidean rhythm construction
- ensemble mode with one orbit per player

### 7. Resonance Labyrinth

A navigable acoustic puzzle where tones excite or quiet simulated spaces. Nodes,
standing-wave patterns, filtering, and sympathetic resonance become mechanics
grounded in real acoustics and clearly labeled models.

Remixes:

- find a room mode by moving a microphone avatar
- tune coupled resonators
- route a signal through filters by ear
- compare waveform, spectrum, and physical motion
- cymatics-inspired visuals labeled as artistic simulations when they are not
  direct measurements

### 8. Melody Flight

Pitch controls altitude, but the path adapts to the player's calibrated range.
Targets begin near a stable comfortable center and teach intervals before absolute
register. Smoothing, dropout grace, and adjustable sensitivity preserve intention.

Remixes:

- sing, whistle, hum, guitar, or keyboard input
- contour-only mode before exact notes
- octave-agnostic interval gates
- harmony flight with a generated companion voice
- free-flight visualizer that saves melodic gestures as MIDI

### 9. Scratch Circuit

Platter motion, crossfader cuts, and cue gestures become a tactile race through
phrases. The DJ Hero controller can become an accessible first deck after its
protocol is identified; touch and mouse gestures preserve a no-hardware path.

Remixes:

- transform, chirp, flare, and orbit gesture families taught generically
- scratch-to-match rhythmic syllables
- crossfader endurance and relaxation exercises
- sample position memory
- scratch gestures that modulate visual geometry

### 10. Rhythm Architect

Players construct musical machines from clocks, dividers, probability gates,
delays, and logic. Every component is both audible and inspectable, joining
sequencer play with mathematical intuition.

Remixes:

- build a backbeat from one master pulse
- derive tuplets with clock division
- make evolving rhythms with probability
- route accents through logic gates
- export the finished machine as a loop and MIDI clip

## Cross-Genre Mechanics Worth Transforming

- **Memory games** become phrase recall with shrinking visual support.
- **Matching games** become timbre, interval, cadence, or sample-family sorting.
- **Platformers** become pitch contour, rhythmic momentum, and syncopation.
- **Racing games** become tempo control where consistency beats raw speed.
- **Fighting-game combos** become rudiments, chord shapes, and scratch phrases.
- **Stealth games** become silence, dynamics, restraint, and negative-space study.
- **City builders** become arrangement, orchestration, and frequency management.
- **Deck builders** become constrained sound palettes and production strategies.
- **Roguelikes** become seeded crate digs with portable, replayable challenges.
- **Dance games** become full-body pulse and gesture with accessibility mappings.
- **Conducting games** become tempo, dynamics, entrances, and ensemble balance.
- **Pinball** becomes bouncing delay, accents, and probabilistic sequencing.
- **Marble runs** become signal flow and modular synthesis.
- **Rubik-like puzzles** become invertible rhythm and harmony transformations.
- **Sandbox games** become loop worlds where every object is a musical behavior.

## Universe of Vibration Rooms

These rooms should distinguish measured science, mathematical model, historical
metaphor, and artistic interpretation.

- Overtone telescope: zoom from fundamental to partials and resynthesize the view.
- Lissajous studio: steer phase and frequency ratios with a controller.
- Chladni table: compare simulated nodal forms with captured reference examples.
- Wave interference pond: place sources and hear constructive/destructive regions.
- Temperament atlas: hear how tuning systems reshape intervals and beating.
- Sonic geometry loom: weave rhythm ratios into visual tilings and playable loops.
- Color-sound mapper: let users author mappings without claiming universal
  correspondence.
- Resonant object museum: strike modeled materials and inspect decay spectra.
- Breath and pulse room: connect tempo perception to paced performance without
  making medical claims.
- Scale solar system: organize pitch collections spatially, then improvise through
  their gravitational relationships.

## A Connected Arcade

Modes should hand material to one another:

1. SetScope recognizes or the user imports a musical moment.
2. Audio Lab measures a safe, trustworthy input.
3. Beat School or Pitch Gates isolates one learnable relationship.
4. A remix mode transforms the learned phrase.
5. Loop Studio saves the original creation.
6. The performance receipt updates the Skill Constellation.
7. Next Move recommends a complementary challenge, not more of the same.

The result is one instrument-shaped world: listening creates lessons, lessons
create performances, and performances create new music.

## Best First Three

1. **Pocket Highway**: directly extends the normalized MIDI foundation and
   hip-hop Beat School.
2. **Sample Alchemy**: connects Rhythm Roulette to original creation.
3. **Harmony Blocks**: establishes the later keyboard/theory expansion with a
   mechanic that is visual, audible, and genuinely different.
